## Shared scene structure

Arrival: show the community stake before offering a match. Explain why this person
chooses competition rather than assuming everyone is a combat vendor. Invite the
player to talk, inspect rules, accept, defer or leave. Display partner consent and
readiness. A refusal is a complete dialogue outcome, not a softlock or an insult.

During: the opponent follows the disclosed rules, recognizes rescue/support actions
and stops when an agreed safety condition fires. Hostile story factions may break
rules only through authored consequences and readable escape/defense objectives.

After: include distinct victory, loss, draw, surrender and refusal dialogue. A duel
does not settle factual truth, confer romantic affection or transfer an Eco-Kin.
Record the actual resolution in the existing mission/Chronicle service. Repeat
matches do not repeatedly grant first-clear rewards or reset a character's growth.

## Sample exchange — Tessa's exhibition

Tessa: "The sponsor paid for the repair crew. They didn't pay for the right to keep us here."
Player choices: "Check your partner first." / "What are the match rules?" / "We can wait."
If the partner withdraws, Tessa stops. The player may help her face the sponsor,
propose a later exhibition or leave. Winning the subsequent match never substitutes
for that choice. Campaign placement remains subject to the Grand Crucible timeline.

## Team authoring contract

Each encounter needs approved EcoKinID plus individual Echoprint/instance references,
consent/health/rest records, an authored role mix, techniques and fallback behavior.
Choose ecological partners appropriate to the final location; do not invent teams
from historical names or assign placeholder IDs into the protected Dex. Difficulty
changes planning, reaction time and objective pressure before hidden stat inflation.
