## 1. Canon principle: one historical spine, many temporal wounds

Rebearth's authoritative historical sequence remains:

1. **First Chorus**
2. **Meridian Age**
3. **War on Humanity**
4. **Great Collapse**
5. **Rebearth Awakening**
6. **Star Rewrite**

The **Aurelian Hierarchy / Great Somatic Fracture** belongs to far-cosmic antiquity outside this local six-era numbering.

The older phrase **Spacetime Rift Era** is retired as a replacement for Era 4. The corrected relationship is:

> **The Great Collapse created a planet-wide network of Chrono-Rift scars.**

Those scars expose preserved moments, damaged records, alternate projections, frozen events, ancient simulations, and future possibility states.

Time travel is therefore a story about **preserving memory and repairing causality**, not an excuse to erase consequences.

### Layer A — Far-Cosmic Antiquity

The **Aurelian Hierarchy** develops prohibited body-state recording, dimensional navigation, Resonance manipulation, consciousness-link systems, stasis architecture, and temporal observation technology.

Meridian researchers discover Heart-Code, Nodes, Tidegates, ecological machines, archive lattices, and early **Chrono-Phase** observation methods.

### Layer D — War on Humanity

Temporal research is militarized into time-locks, causal shields, stasis vaults, archive weapons, and restricted battlefield systems.

### Layer E — Great Collapse

Damaged Nodes, dimensional gates, Resonance infrastructure, and ancient temporal devices interact catastrophically.

Localized **Chrono-Rift scars** form across Rebearth.

### Layer F — Rebearth Awakening

A.E.G.I.S. identifies Chrono phenomena as hazardous Time-Echo anomalies. The player gradually learns to stabilize and investigate them.

### Layer G — Star Rewrite

Restored Riftways reveal that Rebearth's temporal scars intersect with a much older cosmic network, including Aurelian ruins and distant civilizations.

## 7. Causality classes

Every Chrono mission must declare one class.

### C1 — Immutable Anchor
The final event is locked canon. The player can protect it from anomaly interference but cannot change its outcome.

### C2 — Lost Detail
The broad event is known; the mission recovers missing truth.

### C3 — Contested Record
Archives disagree. The mission reveals what actually happened.

### C4 — Future Projection
A possible future generated from the current world state. It can still be changed because it has not happened.

## 15. Local rewind gameplay

A short-duration rewind ability may exist as a **localized combat/utility mechanic**.

It is not the same thing as campaign time travel.

### Allowed rewind scope

A rewind-capable actor may restore a bounded subset of recorded state such as:

- transform;
- velocity;
- selected health/energy state;
- approved status effects;
- authored cooldown markers;
- temporary destructible-object state.

### Forbidden rewind scope

Rewind cannot automatically restore:

- consumed economy transactions;
- traded/sold items;
- quest rewards;
- server-ledger inventory;
- permanent world-state commits;
- Kindling history;
- Dex registration;
- breeding/growth outcomes;
- other players' authoritative state without explicit multiplayer design.

## 16. Time-rewind C++ snippet audit

The pasted `UERTimeRewindComponent` is **REFERENCE / NEEDS REWRITE**, not production-ready.

Problems include:

- `HealthSnapshot` is populated from movement speed rather than health;
- `EnergySnapshot` is never assigned;
- `TDoubleLinkedList` is allocation-heavy for a fixed temporal ring and is not an actual circular buffer;
- no physics restoration after `DisableComponentsSimulatePhysics()`;
- no collision, movement-mode, velocity, attachment, montage, ability, status, cooldown, or authoritative state capture;
- no server authority or anti-duplication boundary;
- no multiplayer correction model;
- no actor-destruction/spawn handling;
- no deterministic sampling timestamp/index;
- no capacity derived safely from floating-point settings;
- rewinding by one cached entry per frame creates frame-rate-dependent rewind speed;
- no memory/performance budget;
- no Unreal Automation tests or packaged runtime evidence.

### Correct implementation direction

Use a preallocated ring buffer per rewind-enabled actor, not all 125 permanent species globally.

Only active actors inside a Chrono-enabled encounter should record snapshots.

Recommended production structure:

- fixed-capacity `TArray<FChronoSnapshot>`;
- integer write cursor;
- server-authored timestamp/frame index;
- explicit capture policy;
- gameplay-tag whitelist for rewindable effects;
- inventory/economy exclusion;
- deterministic playback rate;
- post-rewind physics/movement restoration;
- replicated final authoritative result rather than blindly replaying client history.

This enters implementation only after the real UE5.8 project foundation exists.
