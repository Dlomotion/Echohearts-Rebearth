## Goal

Turn manuscript collecting into a connected investigation across Echohearts: Rebearth instead of a loose collectible hunt.

The player gradually reconstructs two histories at the same time:
- how Eco-Kin lived, migrated, bonded and responded to ecological change;
- what actually happened during the War on Life and how competing groups changed Rebearth.

The two collections repeatedly intersect so the War is understood not only through soldiers and leaders, but through damaged habitats, displaced communities and Eco-Kin behavior.

## Mission Chain

### Mission 1 — Pages Beneath the Roots

**Function:** tutorial / Academy-to-field bridge.

The player is taught that damaged records can contain ecological information that modern systems no longer possess.

Objectives:
1. follow environmental signs to a ruined field station;
2. recover one physical Eco-Kin manuscript page;
3. inspect habitat evidence nearby;
4. compare the page against current Eco-Kin behavior;
5. return the reconstructed entry to the Sanctuary archive.

Payoff:
- unlocks the Eco-Kin Manuscript collection;
- adds the first expanded EcoDex knowledge field;
- teaches that written records can be incomplete or outdated.

### Mission 2 — What the Wild Remembered

**Function:** ecological investigation.

A set of old field manuscripts describes an Eco-Kin population that should still be present, but the habitat now behaves differently.

Objectives:
1. recover multiple field notes from separate micro-habitats;
2. observe current Eco-Kin behavior without treating them as inventory targets;
3. identify where the old record is wrong or incomplete;
4. restore one damaged environmental condition;
5. document the new evidence.

Payoff:
- manuscript entry changes from **Unverified** to **Corroborated** or **Disputed** depending on evidence;
- a hidden route, habitat clue or restoration lead becomes available;
- demonstrates that the player can correct history instead of merely collecting it.

### Mission 3 — The First Redactions

**Function:** introduction to the War on Life archive.

The player discovers a damaged official report whose missing sections conceal why a local region was sacrificed, abandoned or controlled during the War.

Objectives:
1. recover the official report;
2. locate one civilian/survivor account from the same event;
3. locate environmental evidence at the site described in both records;
4. compare contradictions;
5. preserve both records in the archive instead of replacing one with the other.

Payoff:
- unlocks the War on Life Manuscript collection;
- introduces **Disputed** and **Partially Corroborated** archive states;
- opens the first historical-investigation side objective.

### Mission 4 — Ink on the Front

**Function:** battlefield / faction-history investigation.

The player follows a trail of journals, damaged orders and field maps through a former War on Life conflict zone.

Objectives:
1. recover a field journal;
2. reconstruct a damaged movement/order map;
3. investigate a location that the official record labels incorrectly or incompletely;
4. confront present-day danger created by the old decision;
5. restore or stabilize the affected local system.

Payoff:
- reveals how a strategic decision created a lasting ecological consequence;
- updates regional history and world-context dialogue;
- may reveal a path to a hidden relic, memorial or restoration objective.

### Mission 5 — Names the War Buried

**Function:** human and ecological consequence mission.

The player finds fragmented evacuation, missing-person and habitat-displacement records.

Objectives:
1. recover names/records from several connected locations;
2. identify what happened to at least one displaced community and one affected Eco-Kin population;
3. locate a memorial, refuge or surviving trace;
4. return the records so those losses are not reduced to anonymous statistics.

Payoff:
- creates a memorial/history entry in the archive;
- changes selected NPC dialogue or regional context;
- reinforces that restoration cannot erase what happened.

### Mission 6 — The Broken Ledger

**Function:** systems/conspiracy investigation.

Multiple manuscripts reveal that resources, research or restoration technology were redirected during the War.

Objectives:
1. collect records from different authors/sides;
2. reconstruct the timeline of resource movement;
3. identify the location where the diverted material or research ended up;
4. enter the location and deal with the present-day consequence;
5. preserve evidence for later story use.

Payoff:
- connects historical writing directly to exploration and gameplay;
- can unlock a restoration technique, route, crafting/research clue or later mission dependency when approved by the relevant systems team;
- does not automatically declare one faction wholly truthful.

### Mission 7 — The Circle Remembers

**Function:** collection capstone / late-game historical reconstruction.

The player has enough Eco-Kin and War manuscripts to connect ecological evidence, witness accounts, faction records and world-state scars into a coherent timeline.

Objectives:
1. review several previously disputed records;
2. revisit at least one changed/restored region;
3. locate a final corroborating record or environmental proof;
4. complete a War on Life timeline branch;
5. unlock the archive’s final contextual synthesis for that branch.

Payoff:
- the player understands a major War on Life truth because they investigated it, not because a narrator explained it;
- selected late-game dialogue and mission context can acknowledge the completed research;
- archive completion may award a lore-linked cosmetic/display reward, never required combat power.

## Regional Repeatable Pattern

Each major War on Life region may use a smaller three-record structure:

1. **Before** — what the place/Eco-Kin/community was like before the conflict;
2. **During** — a record created while the crisis or battle was happening;
3. **After** — evidence of what survived, changed, migrated, healed or remained wounded.

Finding all three creates a regional historical set with an authored payoff.

## Manuscript Types for Writers

Use variety rather than repeating journals only:
- naturalist field book;
- caretaker notes;
- hand-drawn habitat map;
- field medic notes;
- engineering log;
- research notebook;
- evacuation list;
- letter never delivered;
- fragmented command order;
- memorial record;
- propaganda leaflet;
- censored report;
- survivor testimony;
- restoration log;
- damaged school/archive text;
- pictographic Eco-Kin observation record.

## Writing Rules

- Each manuscript needs a clear author perspective and reason for existing.
- Avoid lore dumps written for the player’s convenience.
- Let records contradict each other where appropriate.
- Use the world itself to verify or challenge text.
- Keep Eco-Kin agency visible.
- Do not make every war record military; civilians, researchers, caretakers, builders and displaced communities matter.
- Avoid clean moral simplification. The War on Life should reveal fear, control, survival, exploitation, sacrifice, restoration and accountability.
- Do not use a manuscript to overwrite locked canon without continuity review.

## Vertical Slice Version

The first vertical slice only needs a compact three-page mini-arc:

### Page A — Eco-Kin Field Note
Describes the normal behavior of the local Eco-Kin and one environmental condition it depends on.

### Page B — War Fragment
Reveals that the condition was deliberately or accidentally disrupted during a War on Life event.

### Page C — Restoration Record
Provides the missing clue that helps the player restore the local system.

The three-page set demonstrates:
- exploration;
- found narrative;
- EcoDex integration;
- environmental puzzle solving;
- War on Life context;
- visible world restoration;
- archive UI.
