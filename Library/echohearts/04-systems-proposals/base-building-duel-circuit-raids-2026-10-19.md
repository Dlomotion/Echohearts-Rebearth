**On the pasted catalog:** declined as a blueprint. It's Palworld's systems with nature find-replace, not methods — and several entries break hard canon: the "Anima Condenser" fuses duplicates (sacrifice — never), "Anima Spheres" are capture items (capture language — never), the Echo-Core's fast travel is teleportation (never), breeding grounds aren't our game, and "Cruel" work settings plus the SAN stat aren't our values or our attributes. **Crafting** uses the existing three tiers (Warden's Bench → Chordwork Atelier → Sovereign Forge) — not duplicated here. **Placement rules:** structures place within the Spire's radius, cost shed/gifted resources (never anything taken by force), and upgrade along two paths — **Deepen** (stronger effect) or **Widen** (larger radius / more served). No structure is required for main progression.

## The 23 structures — every description

### 1. Heartroot Spire (Anchor)
**What it looks like:** A colossal heart-tree grown from the first seed the Tamer ever planted — bark the color of old honey, leaves that chime faintly when the wind moves through them, roots that visibly pulse with slow green-gold light. At its crown, a hollow where the Tamer's A.E.G.I.S. cradle docks. It is the first thing visitors see and the last thing raiders want to face.

**What it does:** The Spire is the base's living center and its beating heart. It sets the build radius — every other structure must stand within its root-reach. It projects the resonance ward, a soft dome that calms wild encounters inside the base (fewer ambushes, gentler first meetings). And it is the companion hearth: from the Spire you choose which companions roam, which work, and which rest. (No fast travel — travel is flight or ride, always.)

**Mechanics:** Build radius 60m at tier 1. Ward softens wild-encounter aggression inside the radius. Companion management hub.

**Upgrades:** *Deepen* — the ward hardens (wild encounters inside become genuinely peaceful; the dome resists more raid damage). *Widen* — the root-reach grows (build radius expands; more companions can be managed at once).

**If it falls:** the base is overrun. Rebuild it first — everything else follows.

### 2. Mossrest Hollow (Rest)
**What it looks like:** A low dome woven from glowing spirit-moss, cool and dim inside, smelling of rain. The moss breathes — slowly brightening and dimming like a sleeping animal. Small companions burrow into it the way cubs burrow into a parent's flank.

**What it does:** The everyday bed. Companions assigned to rest here recover strain overnight and wake clear-headed. It's the workhorse of the care loop: cheap, humble, always full.

**Mechanics:** Restores Energy per hour rested (scales with tier). Clears Weary once Energy rises above zero.

**Upgrades:** *Deepen* — faster recovery, deeper sleep (also eases mild Shaken). *Widen* — more nests, more sleepers at once.

### 3. Bloomrest Loft (Rest)
**What it looks like:** An elevated loft draped in enchanted flowering vines, swaying gently ten feet off the ground. Petals drift down constantly. Companions climb the vine-ladders at dusk without being told.

**What it does:** The healer's bed. Faster strain recovery than the Hollow, and the vine-sap in the air mends minor ailments while companions sleep — small cuts, coughs, the weariness that rest alone doesn't touch.

**Mechanics:** Higher Energy recovery than Mossrest; slowly treats minor Ailing during rest (does not replace the Mender for real sickness).

**Upgrades:** *Deepen* — the sap runs stronger (treats deeper ailments). *Widen* — more lofts, plus room for medium companions.

### 4. Elderrest (Rest)
**What it looks like:** A hollowed ancient root, older than the base, lined with velvet grass and lit by a single shaft of green light from a crack above. It smells of earth and time. The giants duck their heads to enter — and then they sigh, because finally something fits.

**What it does:** Rest for giant guardians who fit nowhere else. Bastodon-sized companions can't use nests and lofts; the Elderrest is built at their scale, with all the same recovery.

**Mechanics:** Full rest recovery for large/Colossal companions. Required for giants to recover strain at base at all.

**Upgrades:** *Deepen* — the old root remembers deeper sleeps (faster recovery, eases Shaken). *Widen* — fits two giants, or one truly enormous one.

### 5. Harvest Basin (Nourishment)
**What it looks like:** A hollowed stone basin, broad as a cart, kept filled with food by whoever's on tender duty — roasted roots, fruit, grain cakes. Steam rises in the morning. Companions eat from it the way travelers eat from a roadside inn: without asking, without ceremony.

**What it does:** The base feeds itself. Hungry companions eat automatically from the Basin — no micromanagement, no one goes hungry because you forgot. A Harvest Tender keeps it filled; an empty Basin is the first sign the workforce is thinning.

**Mechanics:** Auto-feeds companions (consumes stored food). Empty Basin → companions lose Energy faster and morale dips.

**Upgrades:** *Deepen* — richer food (small Energy bonus on eating). *Widen* — feeds more companions, holds more.

**Synergy:** Harvest Tender job; Shed Meadow and Timber Ring supply it.

### 6. Cleansing Font (Purification)
**What it looks like:** A still basin of font-water, perfectly clear, set in a ring of white stones. The water never ripples unless something unclean enters it — then it clouds, and the Purifier knows. Steam doesn't rise; light does, faintly, from below.

**What it does:** Early corruption can be walked back here — not burned out, not cut out, *walked* back. A Tainted companion soaks in the font-water through slow rites while the Purifier hums the old songs. It takes time. It is gentle. It works — for the early stages.

**Mechanics:** Soaking rites reduce corruption stage (Tainted → Clean) over hours. No force, no pain. Requires a Watershaper (Torrent/Glaze companion) cycling the waters for full effect.

**Upgrades:** *Deepen* — deeper water, deeper cleansing (works on heavier Taint). *Widen* — more soakers at once.

**Canon note:** purification, never punishment. The Font is why "walk them back" is a quest chain.

### 7. Thornward Palisade (Defense)
**What it looks like:** Living bramble walls, twelve feet high, woven from blackthorn that flowers white in spring. The thorns are honest — they snag, they slow, they do not kill. After a fight the wall looks ragged; by morning it has already begun to reweave itself.

**What it does:** The base's first line. Invaders are snagged and slowed at the walls, buying the defenders time. It's alive, so it heals — regrowing after every fight without spending resources.

**Mechanics:** Contributes defense rating. Slows raider advance (waves arrive staggered). Regrows HP automatically between raids.

**Upgrades:** *Deepen* — thicker, taller, thornier (more rating, longer slow). *Widen* — longer walls, covering more approaches.

### 8. Seedfall Battery (Defense)
**What it looks like:** A cluster of tall pod-flowers, each head heavy with hard seed-pods, angled at the sky like mortars. During raids, assigned companions climb the firing platforms, laughing — because firing seed volleys at raiders is, objectively, joyful. The air smells of green powder and victory.

**What it does:** Companion-loaded artillery. During raids, assigned companions fire seed volleys at hostile forces — stunning, scattering, and thoroughly demoralizing raiders who expected a quiet village. Loud, joyful, effective.

**Mechanics:** During raids, each assigned companion deals periodic damage/stun to a wave. Needs crew to fire — uncrewed, it's just flowers.

**Upgrades:** *Deepen* — bigger pods, harder hits. *Widen* — more launchers, wider coverage.

### 9. Resonant Bulwark (Defense)
**What it looks like:** A single tuned crystal, tall as a person, humming at a frequency you feel in your teeth. When raids come, the hum deepens and the Spire's ward visibly brightens — the dome goes from soft light to something with edges.

**What it does:** Amplifies the Spire's ward during raids. The dome hardens when it matters: raider damage to structures drops, and the ward itself pushes back lightly against the assault.

**Mechanics:** During raids, multiplies the ward's protection (structure damage reduction). Contributes defense rating passively.

**Upgrades:** *Deepen* — stronger amplification (more damage reduction). *Widen* — the hardened dome covers more of the base.

**Synergy:** the reason high defense rating can break a raid before it starts.

### 10. Stormrod (Power)
**What it looks like:** A tall ironwood conductor, copper-veined, crowned with a glass bulb that holds captured lightning. Before rain it hums — a rising note every child in the base learns to recognize. During storms it glows, drinking the sky.

**What it does:** Drinks storm energy and feeds it to the advanced forges. The Chordwork Atelier and Sovereign Forge don't run without power, and the Stormrod is how the base makes its own — no fuel, no fire, just weather.

**Mechanics:** Generates power charges during storms; stored power runs the Atelier and Forge. The hum-before-rain is a real weather tell.

**Upgrades:** *Deepen* — drinks deeper (more charge per storm). *Widen* — powers more structures at once.

### 11. Timber Ring (Harvest)
**What it looks like:** A ring of rapid-growth trees — ironwood, songbirch, lantern-pine — planted in concentric circles. The inner ring is always being harvested; the outer ring is always regrowing. From above it looks like a green fingerprint.

**What it does:** Sustainable wood. Companions harvest the inner ring while the outer ring regrows — the Ring is never permanently deforested. Take, and take again next season, forever.

**Mechanics:** Yields wood per harvest cycle. Cannot be depleted — the regrowth math guarantees it.

**Upgrades:** *Deepen* — denser wood, rarer grains (better crafting materials). *Widen* — more rings, more yield.

### 12. Stoneheart (Harvest)
**What it looks like:** A crystal node with a slow pulse — a heartbeat you can see. It yields stone and gems from its outer shell, and the shell regrows hourly, layer by layer. The Purifier checks it the way a farmer checks the sky.

**What it does:** The base's quarry that never runs dry. Take the shell, never the heart: the pulse is the promise that there will be more tomorrow.

**Mechanics:** Yields stone/gems hourly up to the shell's regrowth. Overharvesting (taking faster than regrowth) is impossible — the node simply stops giving until the shell returns.

**Upgrades:** *Deepen* — deeper veins (rarer gems). *Widen* — larger node, more per cycle.

### 13. Shed Meadow (Harvest)
**What it looks like:** A peaceful pasture of soft grass where companions go to simply *be*. Feathers, silk, down, and molted scales gather in the grass like dew. Nobody takes anything from anyone here — the meadow collects what is freely left behind.

**What it does:** Happy companions shed resources naturally into the grass — the crafting economy's gentlest input. Given, not taken: sheds only happen when companions are content, so the Meadow is also a happiness meter you can read at a glance.

**Mechanics:** Yields shed/gifted resources (silk, feathers, down, scales) scaled by companion happiness. Unhappy base → empty meadow.

**Upgrades:** *Deepen* — richer sheds (higher star-tier materials). *Widen* — more pasture, more shedders.

**Canon note:** this is the shed/gifted economy made visible. Release ≠ sacrifice, and the Meadow proves it daily.

### 14. Harmony Circle (Morale)
**What it looks like:** A stone ring set with tuned standing stones. When the Bondkeeper strikes the center stone, the ring hums — and the whole base falls into tempo. Work, rest, and play all move to the music.

**What it does:** Music sets the work tempo. Three songs, three honest trades: **Gentle Melody** (slow work, restorative — the tired recover while they work), **Steady Rhythm** (normal pace, normal yield), **Dawn Chorus** (fast work, honest strain cost — more output, more tiredness, never cruel). The Circle never asks for more than the workers can give; Dawn Chorus just asks faster.

**Mechanics:** Active tempo modifies work output and Energy drain. Gentle Melody reduces drain; Dawn Chorus increases both output and drain.

**Upgrades:** *Deepen* — stronger effects, longer songs. *Widen* — the music reaches the whole base (more workers covered).

**Synergy:** Harmonist job (Bondkeeper's aide) keeps the tempos; never a cruel setting — that's the line.

### 15. Soothing Springs (Morale)
**What it looks like:** Steaming mineral pools terraced down a warm rock face, each pool a different heat. Stressed companions seek them out on their own — you'll find a Shaken guardian neck-deep at midnight without anyone telling them to go. The water smells of minerals and relief.

**What it does:** The reset button. Strain drains away in the heat; Shaken nerves unknot; minds clear. Companions self-soothe here — it's the one care structure that needs no assignment, because the needy walk in themselves.

**Mechanics:** Rapid strain/Energy recovery; calms Shaken (valid Soothe source). Self-directed — no job required.

**Upgrades:** *Deepen* — hotter, deeper healing (also eases Ailing mildly). *Widen* — more pools, more soakers.

### 16. Remedy Garden (Morale)
**What it looks like:** An apothecary garden in neat spiraling beds — bitterleaf, sunbalm, dreammint, woundwort. The Mender's Herbhand tends it at dawn, humming. The air is sharp with green medicine.

**What it does:** Brews the base's medicine: salves for injuries, tonics for fatigue, incense for low spirits. The Garden is why the Mender's hands are never empty and why Ailing companions get better instead of just getting rest.

**Mechanics:** Produces remedy items (healing salves, fatigue tonics, calming incense). Required material source for treating Ailing (Remedy care source).

**Upgrades:** *Deepen* — stronger brews (treats deeper Ailing). *Widen* — more beds, more remedies.

**Synergy:** Herbhand job (Mender's aide) boosts output; Flora affinity doubles it.

### 17. Watcher's Perch (Defense)
**What it looks like:** A high nest of woven skywood perched atop the tallest tree near the base, lined with soft down and ringed with far-seeing lenses. The Scout's lookouts trade shifts here; a flying companion often shares the nest, because two kinds of eyes see further than one.

**What it does:** Sees trouble coming. Extends raid warning time — more seconds to harden the Bulwark, ring the Bell, and take positions. Warning is the cheapest defense there is, and the Perch sells it wholesale.

**Mechanics:** +warning seconds per tier. Spotter job (flying/Aero companion aide) extends it further.

**Upgrades:** *Deepen* — sees further (much longer warning). *Widen* — wider watch (also spots hidden-mission phenomena, treasure-hunt glints).

### 18. Resonance Bell (Defense)
**What it looks like:** A great tuned bell hung in a wooden frame at the base's center, cast from resonant bronze that holds a note for a full minute. When it rings, every head turns — residents, companions, even the Spire's leaves seem to listen.

**What it does:** The rally. When the Bell rings, residents move to defense positions instantly instead of trickling in one by one. Seconds matter in a raid; the Bell buys them all at once.

**Mechanics:** On raid warning, instant resident rally (no trickle delay). Also rallies companions to Guard posts.

**Upgrades:** *Deepen* — the note carries further (also steadies Shaken defenders — courage is audible). *Widen* — covers outlying structures.

### 19. Underroot Vault (Defense)
**What it looks like:** A deep root-cellar vault beneath the Spire's roots, cool and dark, lined with stone and locked with a resonance seal only the Tamer can open. The base's treasures sleep here — and raiders can tear the base apart without ever touching them.

**What it does:** What matters most survives. Stored resources inside the Vault can't be looted when a raid breaches — the war can take your walls, but not your winter stores.

**Mechanics:** Vaulted resources are immune to raid looting. Capacity scales with tier.

**Upgrades:** *Deepen* — deeper vault (more secure, holds rarer materials). *Widen* — more capacity.

### 20. Frolic Field (Play)
**What it looks like:** An open meadow worn into winding paths by a thousand chase games. There are tussle-mounds, a splash-ditch, and a "king of the hill" knoll with a slightly tilted flag. It is the loudest, happiest place in the base, and the grass never quite recovers — proudly.

**What it does:** Active play. Chase games and tussles burn off stress and restore Energy faster than idle rest — and joy earns a little Kindling bump, because happiness is bond. Tired workers come here and leave laughing.

**Mechanics:** Play restores Energy at 1.5× the rest rate; small Kindling gain per session. Valid Weary/Shaken care (Play source).

**Upgrades:** *Deepen* — bigger games, bigger joy (more Kindling). *Widen* — more room, more players.

### 21. Chime Grove (Play)
**What it looks like:** Wind chimes and resonance toys strung through a grove of silverbirches — glass bells, tuned reeds, things that spin and sing. The grove is never silent; even still air finds something to stir. Shaken companions come here and the grove hums their nerves quiet.

**What it does:** Musical play that calms. The chimes' frequencies are tuned to settle stressed nervous systems — Shaken companions recover here faster than anywhere else, and even the merely tired leave lighter.

**Mechanics:** Best Shaken recovery in the game (Soothe source). Gentle Energy restore. The grove's hum is audible across the base — a living calmness meter.

**Upgrades:** *Deepen* — deeper tuning (calms faster, reaches deeper strain). *Widen* — more chimes, more soothed at once.

### 22. Splash Hollow (Play)
**What it looks like:** Shallow sun-warmed pools linked by giggling runnels, ringed with smooth stones worn by a thousand splashes. On hot days it's the busiest place in the base. The water is always the perfect temperature — the Glaze-essence companions see to that, smugly.

**What it does:** Water play for the heat-tired and dust-covered. Cool, splashy, restorative — the fastest Energy recovery of the three play stations, with none of the Field's rowdiness. The quiet joy option.

**Mechanics:** Fastest Energy restore among play stations. Valid Weary care. Torrent/Glaze companions get an affinity bonus here (they run the temperatures).

**Upgrades:** *Deepen* — mineral-rich waters (also eases minor Ailing). *Widen* — more pools.

### 23. Mendward (Rest — the hospital)
**What it looks like:** A long, calm hall of pale wood and white linen, smelling of sunbalm and clean water. Beds in a row, each with a window. The Mender moves between them quietly. It's the only building in the base where the rule is *nothing is asked of you* — you just get better.

**What it does:** The hospital. Halves Tamer recovery time after a collapse, houses admitted residents until they walk out, and treats Ailing companions with the Mender's full attention. Without it, the fallen wake slower — in the Spire, aching.

**Mechanics:** Tamer recovery × 0.5. Required for resident admission (no Mendward → residents recover in the field, slower, and their jobs stay quiet longer). Mender care source amplifier.

**Upgrades:** *Deepen* — the Mender's art deepens (faster healing, treats worse injuries). *Widen* — more beds, more patients.

## Destruction: go hard or lose (Tay's rule, 2026-10-19)
Tay: *"I'm not playing safe in this game — you either go hard or lose."* The safety nets are off. The line that holds: **structures burn, people don't** — no companion or resident ever dies; the stakes are everything you built.

- **Every structure has hit points.** Damage it and it works worse; at 0 HP it's **destroyed**.
- **Damaged (not destroyed) → repair.** Costs a fraction of the build cost. Quick, if you're fast.
- **Destroyed → rebuild.** Full build cost, from scratch — and tiers reset to 1. Tiers are earned again. Rubble defends nothing: destroyed defense structures stop contributing rating; destroyed rest/morale structures stop recovering strain.
- **Unresolved raid waves break things.** Every interval, each wave you haven't dealt with damages a standing structure. Ignore the raid and watch your base come apart.
- **The Spire falls → the base is overrun.** If the Heartroot Spire is destroyed mid-raid, the raid ends breached immediately. Rebuild the Spire first; everything else follows.
- **Escalation: the societies remember.** Every breached raid makes the next one hit 25% harder (stacking). Win, or the war gets worse.
- **Repairs are always possible; resurrection isn't.** Nothing here is permadeath for the base — but rebuilding a fallen Spire and three walls while the societies escalate is the price of losing.

Hard but fair: the game always warns you (the Perch, the Bell, the Scout), and recovery is always possible. What it never does is pretend losing is free.

## Tired & sick: the care loop (Tay's expansion, 2026-10-19)
Every working companion has **Energy (0–100)**. Work drains it; at 0 they're **Weary** and can't work until rested. Push further and worse follows:

| Ailment | How it's gained | How it's healed | Who heals it |
|---------|-----------------|-----------------|--------------|
| **Weary** | Energy hits 0 | Rest, play, meals, springs | Rest structures, fun stations, campfire |
| **Ailing** (sick) | Working while Weary, or blight exposure (worse if Weary) | Treatment only — play doesn't cure sickness | Mender's hands, Remedy Garden brews, healing meals |
| **Shaken** | Raids, heavy strain, brutal duels | Calming | Chime Grove, Soothing Springs, campfire scenes, Bondkeeper counsel, Gentle Melody |

**Rules of care:**
- The sick don't work. Full stop — the subsystem refuses.
- Right care for the ailment: you can't play away a sickness, and rest alone won't cure one. Ailing needs the Mender, remedies, or meals.
- Healing is never punished and never rushed: ailments clear through care actions, not timers you pay to skip.
- Code: `Companion/CompanionConditionTypes.h` (ailments, care sources, condition struct), `Companion/CompanionCareSubsystem.h/.cpp` (work drain tick, rest/play/treat/soothe, blight + strain exposure).

## The Infirmary: pass out, wake up, base left open (Tay's expansion, 2026-10-19)
The Anima-Link is bidirectional — the partner's pain becomes the Tamer's burden. Push past zero stamina and the Tamer **passes out** and wakes in the hospital. While the Tamer is down, the base is open and vulnerable.

- **Collapse:** stamina hits 0 → `InfirmarySubsystem.CollapseTamer` fires (hooked in `FrequencyTamerCharacter.CheckCollapse`). Recovery time scales with strain debt. The collapse is reported once per fall — no spam, just the one terrible thud.
- **The Mendward** (structure #23): the hospital. Halves Tamer recovery time and houses the fallen. Without it, you wake slower — in the Spire, aching.
- **Vulnerable base:** while the Tamer is down, defense rating × 0.5 (no commander) and raids press 1.5× harder. The residents hold the line alone.
- **Residents get hurt too:** raid injuries admit residents to the ward (`AdmitResident`) — their services wait until they walk out. A hurt Mender can't mend; a hurt Scout can't warn. And their jobs go quiet (see Workforce).
- **Waking up ≠ ready:** the Recovering debuff — stamina drains faster for a while. The body keeps the score.
- Code: `Sanctuary/InfirmarySubsystem.h/.cpp`.

## The Workforce: everybody leans on everybody (Tay's expansion, 2026-10-19)
Tay: *"Each NPC and EcoKin leans on each other and have unique jobs — you need them on base to keep a healthy and wealthy base."* Jobs are the unit of that trust: one resident's craft, one companion's hands. A job is **active** only when a fit companion holds it AND its resident is on their feet — the lean, verified both ways.

### The founding jobs — every description

**Forgehand** (supports the Artificer; Craft) — The forge never sleeps, but it needs hands that aren't afraid of heat. The Forgehand stokes the coals, hauls the ingots, and holds the work steady while the Artificer shapes it. A Pyre or Voltic companion doesn't just help — they *are* the fire, and the craft comes out better for it. *Affinity: Pyre/Voltic → 50% better output.*

**Herbhand** (supports the Mender; Care) — Dawn in the Remedy Garden: the Herbhand gathers bitterleaf and sunbalm with the care of someone picking for a friend, because they are. A Flora companion reads the plants like kin — they know which leaf is ready before the Mender does. Stronger herbs, stronger healing. *Affinity: Flora → 50% better output.*

**Spotter** (supports the Scout; Watch) — Two kinds of eyes see further than one. The Spotter rides the thermals above the Watcher's Perch — a flying companion, an Aero-touched friend — and calls down what the lenses can't reach: dust on the horizon, wrongness in the treeline. Every extra second of warning is bought here. *Affinity: flying/Aero → 50% longer warning.*

**Watershaper** (supports the Purifier; Care) — The Cleansing Font's waters must cycle — slow, endless, patient. The Watershaper moves them: a Torrent companion coaxing the current, a Glaze companion keeping it cold and clear. Without them the Font stills, and still water cleanses nothing. *Affinity: Torrent/Glaze → 50% faster purification.*

**Harvest Tender** (base; Harvest) — The unglamorous sacred work: keeping the Harvest Basin filled, the Timber Ring cut in rotation, the Shed Meadow combed. The Tender feeds the base that feeds everyone. Any willing hands will do — but willing is the requirement.

**Harmonist** (supports the Bondkeeper; Lore/Morale) — The Harmony Circle's tempos need living voices to hold them. The Harmonist sings, claps, stamps — keeps the Gentle Melody gentle and the Dawn Chorus honest. Music made together works harder than music made alone.

**Guard** (base; Defense) — Walks the walls. A sturdy companion on the Thornward Palisade at midnight, watching the dark the way only someone with night-eyes can. Each Guard on post adds real defense rating — the base's walls are living, and so are its watchers.

**Chronicler's Aide** (supports the Chronicler; Lore) — The Chronicler writes the base's story, but the stories belong to the companions. The Aide shares them: the duel that turned, the storm that was weathered, the joke the Scout told. Deeds become Kindling when they're remembered right.

**The loop:** companions take jobs → the base produces → residents' services keep companions healthy → companions keep working. Break either side and it's felt: an empty job is a promise the base can't keep.

- **Affinity:** the right essence for the work = 50% better output. The right hands matter.
- **Base Health (0–100):** active filled jobs, companion energy, standing structures. Low health → shaken nerves, weaker services.
- **Base Wealth/hr:** active job output. Wealth pays for repairs and rebuilds — the war economy.
- **Admitted residents quiet their jobs.** Hurt Mender → no mending, no herbhand output. That's the lean.
- Code: `Sanctuary/WorkforceTypes.h`, `Sanctuary/WorkforceSubsystem.h/.cpp` (assigning a job registers the companion as a worker with the care subsystem — the work is real, and tiring).

## Duel Circuit (recap with detail)
The ranked sparring ladder at the Sanctuary proving grounds — Hum → Chord → Chorus → Symphony. Best-of-three bouts against named rivals (Maela Thorn and Corvin Hale headline the early card, pending Tay's canon call). Real-time, subdual or yield, and losing never costs Kindling — the Circuit is sport, and sport keeps blades sharp. Three bout victories per rank step. The Friday circuit-night is the base's social heartbeat: residents, companions, music, and somebody's rival talking too much.

## Sanctuary Raids (recap with detail)
The societies — and sometimes the blight itself — probe your home. The shape of every raid: **warning** (the Scout's sky-watch, extended by the Perch and Spotter, instant-rally via the Bell) → **waves** (data-driven, arriving on a timer) → **aftermath** (repairs, rebuilds, the Warden track for flawless defenses). Defense rating can break a weak raid before it starts — the raiders see the Bulwark lit and think better of it. Otherwise, you fight: residents beside you, Guards on the walls, the Battery's crews laughing as they fire. Unresolved waves break structures. Breaches escalate. The Spire falling ends it. Go hard or lose.
