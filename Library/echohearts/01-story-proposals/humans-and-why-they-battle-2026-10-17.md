# Humans & Why They Battle (2026-10-17)

**Status:** 01_Story proposal. Plain language. Nothing here overrides locked canon; names marked PROPOSAL are Tay's to veto.

## Who the humans are

- **Frequency Tamers** — the players. Men and women who learned to tune their own frequency until a wild EcoKin chose to answer. A Tamer's strength was never about owning powerful partners — it's about understanding, healing, training, protecting, and resonating with them.
- **Core-Binders** — Tamers who went further: an advanced specialization for those whose bonds run deep enough to hold through fracture, fusion, and the worst the blight can do.
- **Keepers** — not a rank. It's what an aligned EcoKin calls its human. A relational word, earned, never claimed.
- **Sanctuary residents** — the Mender, the Artificer, the Chronicler, the Bondkeeper, the Purifier, the Scout. Humans rebuilding the world one Sanctuary at a time. Most never fight; their battle is restoration.
- **Secret-society agents** — humans who decided EcoKin are instruments: of conquest, of control, of power. They tamper, they dominate, they weaponize. They are why the Tamers cannot only heal.
- **Discordant Tamers** — Tamers who broke faith with the bond itself. They force domination through the Anima-Link, and the link keeps score: their partners corrupt, their frequency curdles. They are the living argument for why the bond must be mutual.

## Why they battle — five reasons, all ours

**1. Restoration.** The world is wounded, and the wounded ones are dangerous. Tamers battle corrupted EcoKin to subdue them — never to kill — so they can be purified and their territories healed. Every raid against a Cataclysm-class threat is surgery, not sport. *(Battle mode: Raid.)*

**2. Defense.** Shadow Beasts and the blight do not negotiate. When they come for a Sanctuary, Tamers stand in front of it — partners beside them, residents behind them. Some fights are simply about who lives through the night. *(Battle mode: Duel, Sanctuary defense.)*

**3. Rites.** Growth Rites demand proof. A Tamer and partner prove themselves in trial combat — against champions, against the wild, against their own limits — because a bond untested is a bond unknown. *(Battle mode: Duel, Sparring as preparation.)*

**4. Opposition.** The secret societies are turning living forces of nature into instruments of conquest. Someone has to stop them, and stopping them means facing their Heralds, their Paragon, and the dominated EcoKin they throw away like tools. This is the war the whole story is about. *(Battle mode: Duel, Raid.)*

**5. The bond itself.** Two Tamers who respect each other cross partners in consensual sparring — strikes pulled, nothing broken — because a bond deepens under pressure the way a blade deepens under the hammer. Training is how Tamers say *I trust you with my worst day.* *(Battle mode: Sparring — builds Kindling, not wounds.)*

## The line none of them cross
Battles end in subdual, yielding, withdrawal, or purification. A fractured one is brought back, not put down. The moment a Tamer fights to destroy a sentient partner rather than save them, they've stopped being a Tamer and started being the thing the story is about defeating.
