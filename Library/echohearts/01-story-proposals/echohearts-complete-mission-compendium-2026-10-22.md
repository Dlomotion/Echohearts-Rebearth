## The rule of this document

**Layer 0 is untouchable.** The original missions from the GitHub canon repo and the builds that followed them are the foundation — nothing in Layers 1–6 replaces, renames, or retires any of them. New material routes *around and between* the originals. Where a new mission and an original mission touch, the original's terms govern. Mercy rules hold at every layer: no EcoKin dies, no companion or resident dies, structures can fall and be rebuilt, forced links are always broken — never used.

## LAYER 6 — The Action & Conflict Engine (NEW, 2026-10-22)

The originals and chronicles carry the heart. This layer carries the *pressure*. One principle drives all of it:

### 6.1 Restoration Provokes Reaction
Healing the world is not free of enemies. Every region restoration threshold crossed (**25% / 50% / 75% / 100%**) raises that region's **Society Pressure**. The secret societies — who profit from a broken Rebearth — answer healing with escalation: at 25% they scout; at 50% they sabotage (blocked passes, poached crystal seams, cut conduits); at 75% they strike (raids on Sanctuaries and Forge districts, forced-link sweeps); at 100% they make one last open play for the region before it slips from their economy forever. Lord Dred's attention rides on top: the higher the world's total restoration, the more personally he intervenes — from Chronicle 20 onward his hand appears directly (a corrupted Heartroot node, a message left in a freed lab, a guardian turned Severe overnight). **Conflict scales with hope, not with level.** Nothing about this breaks the mercy rules: raids damage structures (repair or rebuild — Tay's "go hard or lose" rule), never people; sweeps are answered by breaking forced links, never by destroying the sweepers' partners.

### 6.2 Recurring conflict engines (built subsystems, wired into the mission network)
These action modes already exist in the C++ build (IMPLEMENTED, not compiled). The compendium assigns each a permanent place in the mission flow:
- **Sanctuary Raids** — defense events fired by Society Pressure and the Dred arc; residents rally (Resonance Bell), structures have HP, breaches stack until repaired.
- **Bounty Board (BlightHunt/Bounty)** — the Bondkeeper posts society enforcers and Severe-stage threats; bounties are *resolved*, not collected: break the forced link, free the partner, walk the enforcer out of the region under escort.
- **Duel Circuit & Tournaments** — every region hosts a circuit stop; ◆ Chronicle beats can be interrupted by a circuit challenge (see 6.3). Strain persists between matches; yield is always free; nothing is taken from the loser.
- **Glide Races (GlideRace)** — Skycurrents and Sky Isles race ladders; several Frontier ops and one Chronicle ◆ hang mission-critical chases on race mechanics (pursue a poacher glider to its roost — the cargo is freed, the glider pilot is *outflown*, never shot down).
- **Storm Chases (StormChase)** — forecast-driven: when A.E.G.I.S. calls a hurricane or tornado 24h out, a timed purification run opens inside the storm track. This is the weather system earning its keep.
- **Rift Delves (RiftDelve)** — timed descents into fresh fractures; the corruption inside *spreads while you work*, so purification is a race against a rising number, not a health bar.
- **Elderwing Voyages (Voyage)** — the Elderwing sky-vessel runs supply and evacuation routes; society airships contest them (see Sector 2 Op 7's boarding, now a repeatable voyage class). Boarding actions: grapple-free, flight-only approaches — you land on their deck because your partner carried you there.
- **Rematch Ladder** — any quelled boss may be re-met as an *honorable duel* (their choice, their terms, no corruption at stake) — the action endgame for players who want the fight again without the tragedy.
- **Escort (Escort)** — the standing class for surveyors, herds, medicine, and freed EcoKin moving through contested country; several Layer 0–4 missions are formally Escort-class.

### 6.3 ◆ Action upgrades to existing missions
- **Chronicle 9 (Coastline Horizon)** ◆ — the herd escort becomes a running gauntlet: poacher nets anchor both ends of the beach and a society skiff works the shallows; you free netted EcoKin *while the herd moves*, and the herd's own guardians decide mid-mission whether your pace is trustworthy.
- **Chronicle 13 (Exile / Blightstorm)** ◆ — the Sanctuary breach is a full Raid event (6.2), played, not watched: structures fall in real time, residents hold their posts, and the evacuation order is yours to give or delay.
- **Chronicle 24 (The Herald's Gambit)** ◆ — the three-Sanctuary strike runs *simultaneously*: you defend one personally; the other two are held by the allies and partners you've Kindled across the game — your whole save file is the reinforcement pool. Weak Kindling network, thin defense. The campaign's thesis, weaponized as a difficulty curve.
- **Veridian Nova chain** ◆ — the fire-line trial gains a second front: society arson teams are *feeding* the fire from cache points; splitting your party between the fire line and the caches is the real trial.
- **Dred arc, "Chains"** ◆ — the escape is a moving escort-fight through the lab's service levels with freed EcoKin covering *your* retreat — the rescued rescue the rescuer.
- **Forge Ops 36–42** ◆ — the hurricane is not backdrop: storm-surge flooding changes the district map mid-defense, and the Spire's defense grid must be re-routed as water takes streets.

### 6.4 New action missions (8)
1. **The Red Veil Night** *(Sanctuary, any era after SF_01)* — Collector Silas's promised appearance becomes a night-long cat-and-mouse across the Sanctuary grounds: he is hunting a specific EcoKin's Echoprint for a buyer. No combat with Silas is possible — he never fights. You win by making the Sanctuary's trust network close ranks faster than he can map it. He leaves empty-handed and *impressed* — a recurring antagonist is born.
2. **Breakwater** *(Coralus coast, mid-game)* — a society dam-and-strip operation on a tidal reef: a timed dismantling under a turning tide, Torrent partners holding the water back while you unbuild their works piece by piece.
3. **The Long Haul** *(Great Plains → Wetland Forge)* — a cross-region Escort: the Forge's medicine caravan crosses society country during storm season; GlideRace pursuits, a bridge standoff, and a night raid on the camp, in sequence.
4. **Static Bloom** *(Open Range, Voltic storm event)* — a Voltic super-cell is spawning Hollow-state herds wherever lightning strikes: a StormChase purification circuit ridden at speed, strike point to strike point.
5. **The Undertow Toll** *(Moonlit Bayou)* — society toll-keepers have chained a bayou narrows with forced-link guardian EcoKin. Every guardian freed makes the next one *harder* — the toll-keepers escalate — until the last guardian, a huge old Terra tortoise, chooses on its own to simply walk through the chain.
6. **Ashfall Run** *(Volcanic Ridge, Pyre event)* — an eruption column is dropping ash on a nesting valley: an evacuation GlideRace against the ash front's clock, carrying what can't fly and guiding what can.
7. **The Ninth Bell** *(any Sanctuary, late game)* — a full-scale Raid finale: all surviving societies strike one Sanctuary together during a Resonance Bell test. Every resident, every job assignment, every structure tier you've built is the defense. The compendium's base-building exam.
8. **What the Herald Owes** *(post-Chronicle 24)* — the Society Herald, defeated, requests a *duel* — one Tamer, one partner, circuit rules, no stakes but the truth about who funds the societies. Win or yield, you get the answer; the duel is the interrogation.

### 6.5 Rival Tamers (PROPOSAL — names from Tay's earlier paste, awaiting his approval)
Two recurring duelists threaded through the Circuit and several ◆ beats: **Ren of the Radiant Pulse** (Aura-tempo duelist; beats you fairly and says so; becomes the Circuit's conscience) and **Lyra the Midnight Chord** (Shade-cadence duelist; fights like she's testing whether your Kindling is real — because she is). Neither is a villain. Both are mirrors: Ren shows the player mastery, Lyra shows them doubt. Their duel ladder is the Duel Circuit's human spine between boss purification arcs.

## Addendum (2026-10-22, later) — Salvage from the "Floatia City" paste
The paste itself was a Digimon reskin (named Digimon species, the other game's city/agency/place names, a digital-world premise, the rejected invented regions and Core Matrix) — **none of its content was imported.** Two *methods* survive, restated in canon terms:
1. **Residency unlocks structures.** A wild EcoKin who chooses to reside at a Sanctuary can anchor a specific facility — the Sanctuary grows because of *who chose to live there*, extending the resident-chain pattern (Layer 0.3) to recruited residents. Which EcoKin anchors which structure is a Dex-by-Dex design pass, not an import.
2. **New action bounty — "The Defusal"** *(Wetland Forge, Bounty class, Layer 6.2).* A Synth-Origin colossus fitted with forced-resonance cores is rampaging at the Forge boundary in a frenzy state. It cannot be fought down — the cores must be **defused one by one under pressure** while it moves, each defusal lowering its frenzy until the natural frame underneath can be reached and purified. The cores are Morvane-pattern work: the bounty is also Dred-arc evidence.

## Addendum 2 (2026-10-22, later) — The Hunting Grounds
Tay asked for the missing hunt missions: a real hunting sport. Filed as its own system doc (`files/04-systems-proposals/hunting-grounds-2026-10-22.md`): five licensed Grounds (Great Plains, Deepwood, Frostwind Peaks, Moonlit Bayou, Red Canyons), a full stalk loop (sign → wind/noise/cover approach → resonance tag inside the optimal band, undetected → harvest-card scoring), seasons on the forecast weather system, a Hunt Circuit, the Hunters' Lodge and its Terms, and a 10-mission Hunt chain ending in the Huntmaster's Exam. The adaptation is explicit: the trophy is the tag and the Snap photograph — the EcoKin walks away, remembers the hunter, and may later offer Binding.
