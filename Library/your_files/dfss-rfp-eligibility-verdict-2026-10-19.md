# Eligibility Verdict: Chicago DFSS October 30 Youth-Program RFP

**Verdict: NO-GO on direct application — and the RFP itself could not be verified.**

This is not legal advice. Nothing has been filed or sent anywhere.

## What was checked (2026-10-19)

Three separate searches were run for the described RFP — "Chicago DFSS RFP #67226,"
"DFSS October 30 youth program RFP," and the City of Chicago's current DFSS funding
opportunities. **No RFP matching #67226 or an October 30 youth-program deadline was
found in any public listing.** Chicago DFSS posts open RFPs through the City's
procurement channels; none of the youth-services RFPs currently visible match this
number or deadline.

Because the RFP's published eligibility criteria could not be located, **no
eligibility verdict can be made against its actual rules.** Any go/no-go stated
without the RFP text would be a guess, and this document will not guess.

## The structural problem (independent of the RFP text)

Even if the RFP is located, direct application faces a near-certain barrier:

- **Your foundation is currently Owens Family Foundation LLC** — an Illinois
  limited liability company. The conversion to an Illinois not-for-profit
  corporation is **not approved**, the third director is **unnamed**, and
  purpose/bylaws are **unadopted** (per your 2026-10-08 conversion checklist).
- Chicago DFSS delegate-agency RFPs overwhelmingly require respondents to be
  **not-for-profit agencies with 501(c)(3) status** — e.g., a current City
  human-services solicitation states: *"Eligible bidders must be not-for-profit
  agencies with a 501(c)(3) status."* An LLC mid-conversion does not meet that.
- The October 30, noon CST deadline is 11 days away. Conversion (EOA 205 + NFP
  102.10), board organization, bylaws, and IRS 501(c)(3) determination cannot
  complete in that window.

## The honest alternative: partner route

If the RFP is real and you want in, the viable path is **not** direct application:

1. **Find an eligible lead applicant** — an existing Chicago 501(c)(3) youth-services
   nonprofit willing to serve as lead agency, with your foundation (or studio) as a
   subcontractor/partner delivering the game-dev / tech-training programming.
2. **Or use a fiscal sponsor** — a 501(c)(3) that receives the award and
   subcontracts program delivery to you.
3. Either way, the lead must demonstrate youth-development experience, financial
   strength, and the administrative capacity the RFP requires; your piece is the
   program design (game-dev education for Chicago youth), not the corporate vehicle.

## What would change this verdict

- Locating the actual RFP text (number, posting agency, deadline). If you have a
  link or the posting agency name, it can be read directly and scored line by line.
- Completing the foundation conversion *and* receiving IRS 501(c)(3) determination
  — a months-long process, not an 11-day one.

## Bottom line

**Do not apply directly.** If this grant matters, the next concrete step is finding
the RFP text first, then a partner-or-sponsor conversation — not an application.
