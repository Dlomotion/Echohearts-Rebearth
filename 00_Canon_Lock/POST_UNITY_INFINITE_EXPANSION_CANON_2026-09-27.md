# ECHOHEARTS: REBEARTH — POST-U.N.I.T.Y. INFINITE EXPANSION CANON

**Date:** 2026-09-27  
**Status:** CANON LOCK / POST-CAMPAIGN GROWTH FRAMEWORK  
**Authority:** `MASTER_PROJECT_INDEX.md` → `UNITY_FINAL_MISSION_AND_ENDINGS_CANON_2026-09-27.md` → this file  
**Scope:** Defines what happens after U.N.I.T.Y. and how future sessions/seasons add modes, levels, scenery, systems, Eco-Kin and stories without replacing the original campaign.

## 1. Core rule

**Life is infinite ♾️, and the game world grows with it.**

After the U.N.I.T.Y. finale, Echohearts: Rebearth does not freeze into a completed museum state. Regeneration becomes the in-world explanation for continuing development.

New content may reveal:

- locations that existed in lore but were not explorable in the original launch design;
- landscapes regenerated from damaged or inaccessible regions;
- new settlements built after the wars;
- previously sealed Ancient Tech environments;
- restored underwater, subterranean, sky and orbital zones;
- new off-world destinations reached through the Star Rewrite network;
- new seasonal habitats, weather states and ecological migrations;
- new Eco-Kin introduced in later development sessions and seasons;
- new game modes that use systems already established by the main campaign.

The original launch world remains historically valid. Later content expands it rather than pretending it was always fully accessible.

## 2. Post-U.N.I.T.Y. world-state principle

The postgame is called the **Infinite Regeneration Era** as a working production term until/unless a later session approves a final public-facing era title.

The world enters an ongoing cycle:

**Victory → Healing → Regrowth → Discovery → New Conflict → Cooperation → Transformation → Regeneration → Discovery again.**

This does not create a seventh replacement era for the locked Six Eras unless separately approved. It is a post-campaign continuity layer that can span seasons, DLC and future titles.

## 3. New modes introduced after U.N.I.T.Y.

The following mode families are authorized as post-U.N.I.T.Y. expansion lanes. Exact public names, player counts and balance rules remain subject to implementation and testing.

### A. Restoration Expeditions

Open-ended exploration missions into newly regenerated or previously inaccessible regions.

Primary loops:

- map discovery;
- habitat repair;
- environmental puzzles;
- rescue/research;
- new Eco-Kin observation and Kindling;
- rare material recovery;
- settlement rebuilding;
- dynamic ecology changes.

### B. Chrono-Echo Expeditions

Playable historical or alternate-perspective scenarios accessed through bounded Chrono-Echo Anchoring.

Uses:

- revisit World War / War on Humanity scars;
- later-session Great War campaigns;
- Spacetime Conflict records;
- Summoning War encounters;
- lost civilian stories;
- reconstruction missions showing what happened after famous battles.

These scenarios cannot rewrite C1 Immutable Anchors or duplicate rewards.

### C. Great War Operations

Reserved for future sessions.

This mode family becomes active only after the Great War's detailed canon is authored.

It may include:

- large cooperative battlefield scenarios;
- convoy protection;
- fortification defense;
- evacuation;
- multi-front objectives;
- Ancient Tech recovery;
- faction alliance missions;
- environmental restoration after battle.

The Great War remains a historical bridge into the later Summoning War and U.N.I.T.Y. conflict.

### D. Summoning War Operations

Post-campaign access to major Summoning War records, surviving threats and unresolved summoned entities.

Focus:

- containment versus cooperation;
- rescue of summoned beings;
- faction consequences;
- large-scale encounters;
- cosmic route defense;
- lore linking the Summoning War directly to U.N.I.T.Y.

### E. Resonance Arena / Harmony Circuit Expansion

Competitive and cooperative combat continues as a simulation/sport framework separate from main-campaign morality.

Can expand with:

- new arenas;
- environmental hazards;
- rotating seasonal rules;
- EchoDeck tactical options;
- ranked and unranked formats;
- faction exhibition events;
- accessibility and spectator improvements.

Main campaign remains real-time third-person. Tactical/grid/card systems stay in approved Arena/Harmony Circuit/EchoDeck contexts.

### F. Sanctuary / Homeland Restoration Mode

Long-form rebuilding and ecology management after the final war.

Players can:

- restore farms and habitats;
- expand hospitals and healing incubators;
- rebuild towns;
- invite eligible NPCs;
- create public gardens and memorials;
- establish research stations;
- support voluntary Eco-Kin Partner Assist tasks;
- track regional Purity, Harmony and ecosystem health.

No forced labor, sentient trading, forced breeding or ownership framing is introduced.

### G. Star Rewrite Exploration

The cosmic continuation lane.

Unlocks:

- new worlds;
- Yonderworld cultures;
- Alien-Kin ecosystems;
- Saviors / STARZ* content where approved;
- Ancient Tech Wars;
- Saviors of the Universe;
- Summoning Wars continuation;
- new diplomacy, exploration and restoration stories.

This lane expands beyond Rebearth without making Rebearth irrelevant.

### H. Regeneration Cycle / New Journey+

A replay-oriented mode that allows players to revisit the campaign with retained non-destructive legacy rewards, additional world-state variants and newly authored scenes.

It must not:

- erase prior ending canon;
- duplicate unique rewards;
- overwrite permanent ledger choices;
- turn Chrono systems into unrestricted timeline resets.

## 4. Added levels and scenery missing from the original design

Post-U.N.I.T.Y. content is the authorized place to add environments that were conceptualized, implied or unavailable during the original launch scope.

Examples include:

- newly grown Heartroot forests over former war zones;
- reclaimed cities built inside old ruins;
- repaired Sea-Vein routes;
- expanded Sky Island districts;
- hidden Meridian chambers;
- sealed Aurelian vaults;
- deep-ocean sanctuaries;
- subterranean root civilizations;
- rebuilt Frost Vale settlements;
- new Aryll desert oases;
- post-war memorial fields;
- abandoned Great War fortifications;
- Summoning War crater-zones;
- orbital gardens;
- regenerated fungal caverns;
- new volcanic islands;
- floating ecosystems born from restored gravity systems;
- distant moons and star-route waystations;
- new Yonderworld planets.

### Scenery rule

New scenery should visibly show time passing and life returning.

A location may have multiple temporal/ecological presentations:

1. **War Scar** — damaged, contaminated or abandoned;
2. **Recovery** — active restoration, temporary structures, returning wildlife;
3. **Regenerated** — mature ecosystem and rebuilt communities;
4. **New Growth** — entirely new architecture, species relationships and cultural use built on top of remembered history.

The goal is not merely adding prettier maps. The scenery must communicate the living consequences of player history.

## 5. Launch scope versus infinite scope

The launch/vertical-slice rule remains unchanged:

- prove the game with a small polished slice;
- do not delay release waiting for every Eco-Kin, biome, war, city or mode;
- protect the 125-ID Permanent Dex authority;
- introduce future Eco-Kin through later sessions/seasons;
- add new areas only when they meet design, performance, accessibility and implementation evidence gates.

The **Infinite Expansion** concept is therefore a content-governance rule, not permission for uncontrolled feature creep before the core game works.

## 6. Seasonal regeneration structure

A season can introduce a bounded package such as:

- one regenerated or newly discovered region;
- one major storyline or historical thread;
- a small group of Eco-Kin releases/forms;
- one mode update;
- one faction development;
- new environmental scenery/state;
- one system refinement;
- cosmetics and optional challenges;
- restoration consequences that remain visible afterward.

A season should leave a permanent historical trace instead of resetting the universe back to zero.

## 7. Connection to U.N.I.T.Y.

U.N.I.T.Y. is the doorway into the Infinite Regeneration Era.

The true ending proves that cooperation made survival possible. The postgame asks the next question:

> **Now that everyone survived together, what can they build together?**

This makes the ending a beginning.

The final continuum becomes:

**WAR → U.N.I.T.Y. → HEALING → REGENERATION → NEW MODES → NEW LEVELS → NEW SCENERY → NEW ECO-KIN → NEW WORLDS → NEW GENERATIONS → ♾️**

## 8. Continuity safeguards

Future content must:

- preserve prior canon unless explicitly revised through the established governance process;
- retain consequences of earlier wars and endings;
- avoid one-to-one copies of outside franchises or game modes;
- keep Eco-Kin autonomous living beings;
- preserve Kindling/consent/restoration ethics;
- route through the existing folders and Master Project Index;
- distinguish design intent from VERIFIED implementation;
- never claim a new level/mode is playable until build/runtime evidence exists.

## 9. Closing canon statement

**Echohearts: Rebearth is designed to keep growing because life keeps growing.**

The original game tells the first complete story. U.N.I.T.Y. closes that story's war, but regeneration opens the next horizon.

> **“The world we saved is not the world we stop at. It grows.”**
